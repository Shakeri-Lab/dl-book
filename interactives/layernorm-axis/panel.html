Scope and caveats

The separate CNN sketch holds one channel fixed and pools images and spatial positions. The LayerNorm profile keeps a fixed value ruler; its dashed blue ghost is the raw first token. Each row normalizes separately, before learned scale and shift. Positive epsilon makes the near-unit variance and coincident profiles approximate.

Why BatchNorm fits images and LayerNorm fits sequences

Why sharing can fit a CNN. One convolutional channel measures the same kind of feature across a regular spatial grid. BatchNorm shares that channel's training statistics across images and positions, the \(N,H,W\) axes. Matching image sizes make dense minibatches convenient; fixed image size is not a requirement of BatchNorm itself. By default, evaluation uses running statistics collected during training.

Why token-local statistics fit variable lengths. Ordinary temporal BatchNorm pools the \(B,T\) axes. Unmasked padding enters those statistics; even masked pooling gives longer sequences more entries. LayerNorm instead pools a token's features: sequence length can vary while feature width stays fixed. With that token's input features held fixed, padding elsewhere or different batchmates cannot change its LayerNorm result. It uses the same calculation in training and evaluation. Attention and loss masks are still needed.
