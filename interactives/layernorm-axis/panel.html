Watch the mechanism: BatchNorm down, LayerNorm across

BatchNorm and LayerNorm apply the same formula. If a neighboring token's values change, does this token's normalized output change too?

First compare the groups that share statistics. Then follow the audit's first token as its feature profile shifts and rescales. Last comes a test: a neighboring token doubles. Decide beforehand what will move.

\( \mu=\dfrac1d\class{la-pool-term}{\sum_k}\featurepart{x_k} \) \( \predictionpart{z_k}=\dfrac{\class{la-center-term}{\featurepart{x_k}-\mu}}{\class{la-scale-term}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\epsilon}}} \) \( \operatorname{Var}(\predictionpart{z})=\dfrac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2+\epsilon},\quad\epsilon=10^{-5}>0 \)

Scope and caveats

The separate CNN sketch holds one channel fixed and pools images and spatial positions. The LayerNorm profile keeps a fixed value ruler; its dashed blue ghost is the raw first token. Each row normalizes separately, before learned scale and shift. Positive epsilon makes the near-unit variance approximate. The closing test is a computed variant of the audit tensor, not new data: the neighboring token (40, 50, 60, 70) is doubled and the same LayerNorm is run again. Of BatchNorm, only the pooled feature-1 mean is shown, 10 before and 20 after; no BatchNorm output is computed. While the neighbor's numerals move they are rounded to whole numbers. The test holds this token's own features fixed; attention may already have mixed context into them.

Why BatchNorm fits images and LayerNorm fits sequences

Why sharing can fit a CNN. One convolutional channel measures the same kind of feature across a regular spatial grid. BatchNorm shares that channel's training statistics across images and positions, the \(N,H,W\) axes. Matching image sizes make dense minibatches convenient; fixed image size is not a requirement of BatchNorm itself. By default, evaluation uses running statistics collected during training.

Why token-local statistics fit variable lengths. Ordinary temporal BatchNorm pools the \(B,T\) axes. Unmasked padding enters those statistics; even masked pooling gives longer sequences more entries. LayerNorm instead pools a token's features: sequence length can vary while feature width stays fixed. With that token's input features held fixed, padding elsewhere or different batchmates cannot change its LayerNorm result. It uses the same calculation in training and evaluation. Attention and loss masks are still needed.

Check yourself. Multiply the first token's four features by 10, to (10, 30, 50, 70). What is its LayerNorm output?

The same: (−1.342, −0.447, 0.447, 1.342), up to the tiny effect of ε. The mean and the standard deviation both grow tenfold and cancel. LayerNorm removes each token's own offset and scale, whatever the other tokens are doing.
