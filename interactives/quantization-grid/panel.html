Watch the mechanism: seven ticks for eight weights

Eight weights, seven ticks: two pairs already share a tick. What pulls them apart, and what does that cost?

These eight weights and this 3-bit grid are the left panel of the figure above — values rounded onto grid = np.arange(-3, 4) / 3. Every other number here is computed from them.

b = 3 bits

\( \displaystyle \widehat{w} = \class{qg-s}{s}\,\operatorname{round}(\parameterpart{w}/\class{qg-s}{s}), \qquad \class{qg-s}{s} = \frac{\max|\parameterpart{w}|}{\class{qg-Q}{Q}}, \qquad \class{qg-Q}{Q} = 2^{b-1}-1 \)

Back to three bits: the pairs merge again; payload falls to 0.375 GB.

The calculation is readable without playback. Opening this panel loads the optional controls.

One tensor, one scale: s is set by the largest weight and every weight shares it — which is why ±1.00 are the only two the grid leaves alone, and why at seven ticks two pairs cannot be told apart. Opening the grid is this panel's demonstration, not the chapter's remedy: at 8 bits the same rule gives Q = 127, 255 ticks, s = 0.0079 and no error above 0.0039, but the bits are the price the closing beat writes down — 0.375 GB per billion weights at three bits against 1 GB at eight — and the chapter's own next step is per-row scales and zero-points, not more bits. Those and the affine grid are out of scope here; the 64 × 256 audit whose errors the figure's other two panels report is not reproduced, and nothing on this picture measures it. b is the one control: the timeline moves it, 3 → 8 → 3, and dragging it to 2, 3, 4 or 8 pauses playback and recomputes every tick, dot, bar and number from the chapter's rule. The reconstructions (−1, −0.667, −0.667, 0, 0, 0.333, 0.667, 1), the eight errors, the largest of them 0.127 against the bound s/2 = 0.167, the two collisions, the 8-bit grid and the payload are computed here from the cell's own numbers; the chapter prints none of them. Payload is the chapter's Nb/8 lower bound — bytes of codes, not a checkpoint size and not a speed. The weights are drawn in the colour reserved for a learnable parameter because that is what they are; the grid is a rule, not a thing that learns, and the wine bar is the move the rule makes. Three marks are this panel's own drawing devices and not the chapter's: the dashed ring around a pair that shares a tick; the empty socket that ring becomes when the grid opens, tied by a short dashed line to each of the two dots that left it; and the 8-bit grid drawn as its 255 ticks where the pane can separate them and as a band where it cannot, with the count written once under the picture either way.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. Collide. The picture opens on the answer-in-waiting: the 3-bit grid, seven ticks 0.333 apart from −1.00 to 1.00, and the eight weights already standing on their nearest ticks as orange dots. Two ticks carry two dots each, stacked, and a dashed wine ring draws itself around each stack. Those two ticks are named by what the weights became: −0.667 and 0.
  2. Where they came from. Four open orange ghosts rise at −0.79, −0.54, −0.11 and 0.08, each with its value written above it, and a wine bar grows from each ghost to the tick it landed on. Four different weights, two numbers.
  3. The bound. The four values retire; the other two moved weights, 0.31 and 0.72, take their bars; each tick's basin — everything within s/2 = 0.167 of it — is washed alternately; a bracket measures that half-width from the one tick the dots left free; and 0.127, the longest move on the picture, is written under the bar that is it. s is washed in the formula.
  4. Open the grid. The slider rises from 3 to 8 bits and the basins, the bracket and the bars clear. Seven ticks give way to 255 at a spacing of 0.0079, too fine to draw tick by tick at a phone's width, where the grid becomes a band instead; every dot glides to a tick of its own and the two stacks come apart. Each ring stays behind as an empty socket, tied by a dashed line to the two dots that left it, and the four values relight over their own dots. Q is washed in the formula.
  5. The price. The slider falls back to 3, the grid returns to seven ticks, the dots glide home and re-stack inside the two rings, and the payload line is written: 0.375 GB per billion weights at three bits, against 1 GB at eight. The grid is yours after that: set b to 2, 3, 4 or 8 and every mark is recomputed.
