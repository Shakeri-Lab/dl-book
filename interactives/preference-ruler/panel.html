Watch the mechanism: a preference ruler with no fixed zero

If every reward score increases by the same amount, does a response become more likely to win?

Follow responses A and C from the transitive example above. First read their score difference; then add the audit's common shift to both scores.

\( \predictionpart{P(A\succ C)}=\sigma\!\left(\class{pr-difference}{\predictionpart{r_A}-\predictionpart{r_C}}\right) \) \( =\sigma\!\left((\predictionpart{r_A}+\class{pr-cancel}{c})-(\predictionpart{r_C}+\class{pr-cancel}{c})\right) \)

The common additions cancel. Comparisons identify a difference, not an absolute zero for reward.

The calculation is readable without playback. Opening this panel loads the optional controls.

These are the existing transitive example's scores and common shift, not a new fit. The viewport follows the scores while tick spacing stays fixed; it does not compress the gap. The Bradley–Terry probability models a comparison under one prompt and review process, not correctness or universal value. The constant may depend on the prompt but must be shared by its candidates. This invariance does not generally hold for unequal additions or rescaling. Cyclic judgments still need the separate audit above.
