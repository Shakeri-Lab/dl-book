Watch the mechanism: why a good batch gradient turns useless

A batch of four instead of all twenty. Is its direction simply a worse one — everywhere?

The chapter's own minibatch update, its unbiasedness statement and its batch_size = 4, on the twenty-point schematic dataset this panel declares: five batches, one iterate, one ruler.

The minibatch gradient is unbiased, never a worse gradient; near the optimum the noise does not grow, the signal vanishes, and nothing in this panel is trained.

Scope and caveats

The dataset is schematic: the chapter's eighty points come from torch.manual_seed(6050) and are never printed, so this panel declares twenty of its own — five replicate measurements at each of four x values — on its own line y = −2x + 1.5, which is deliberately not the line Chapter 1's descent panel declares. Every gradient, noise vector, mean, spread, step and ruler reading is computed in the browser from those twenty points. The decomposition, the unbiasedness statement, batch_size = 4 and the two zones are the chapter's.

The region of confusion is about the gradient estimate, not about the loss: it is the exact set where the full gradient is shorter than a typical batch noise vector, and the loss inside it is near its minimum, not confused. Its ellipse is this panel's computed level set; the chapter draws a circle of its own choosing.

The five noise vectors are exactly the same at every (w, b) here because every declared batch carries the same four x values. That rigidity is a property of this declared partition, not a general law: in general a batch's noise varies with the parameters, and only its expectation is pinned to zero.

The arrows are drawn at one declared magnification of the gradient, the same at every time so the shrinking signal is legible; the chapter's figure instead normalises its arrows to unit length in each panel. The step actually taken is the learning rate times the batch gradient — a different, shorter vector that is never drawn.

The batch order is a declared deterministic list: five epochs, each a permutation of the five batches. The chapter prescribes a shuffle, and its own fine print notes that after the first batch moves w, the next batch is no longer an independent unbiased draw at the new iterate.

Five batches stand in for the chapter's twenty. More batches would not narrow the spread; a larger batch would, which is a different scene. The remedy for the milling — letting the step size come down — belongs to the next section and is not shown here. Nothing is trained, no loss curve is plotted, and no measured run appears.

Check yourself. Move the iterate to twice its opening displacement from the optimum: (1.8, −1.8) instead of (0.9, −0.9). Does the spread of the five batch directions open wider or close up?

It closes to about half: the widest arrow sits 5.39° off the true direction instead of 10.19°. The five noise vectors never change; doubling the displacement doubles the full gradient, 2.8814 to 5.7628, so the same sideways push bends a direction half as far.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. Far from the optimum, at (−1.1, 0.6). One wine arrow is the full gradient over all twenty declared points; the ruler under the plane reads its size, 2.8814.
  2. Five batches of four send five thinner arrows from the same point. They spread by at most 10.19 degrees, and every one still points down the bowl.
  3. Each batch arrow is the full one shifted by its own noise vector. The five shifts average to zero, so the dashed outline through the tips is centred exactly on the full arrow's tip; the ruler gains a fixed ink mark at the typical noise size, 0.5561, and the shaded zone below it.
  4. The iterate begins to step, one batch per step, twenty-five steps in five epochs. The full arrow shortens; the outline through the tips keeps its size and shape exactly.
  5. The wine mark on the ruler has slid under the ink one: the full gradient is now smaller than a typical batch noise vector, and the spread has opened past a right angle.
  6. The ellipse where that is true appears round the optimum, the region of confusion. Inside it the five arrows point in five different directions.
  7. The iterate mills inside the ellipse without settling. Its distance from the optimum stays near 0.05 and never falls to zero; the arrows keep disagreeing.
  8. A hollow mark records where the signal started. The full gradient has fallen by a factor of about twenty-two while the ink noise mark has not moved at all. Letting the step size come down is the next section's business, not this panel's.
