Check yourself. Same forward pass, same cache. What is the slope of the loss with respect to the bias b?

0.168900. The first two backward factors are reused unchanged, 0.821899 × 0.205500; only the last edge differs, because z changes by 1 per unit of b instead of x = 2 per unit of w. It is exactly half of the slope for w.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. One line, four nodes: w is a dial; z, a and L follow. Three fixed numbers sit on the edges: x = 2, b = −0.5, y = 0.3. Only w turns. Before playing: turn w up by 0.01 — does L rise or fall, and by how much per unit of w?
  2. Forward, left to right. w = 0.700 gives z = 0.900, then a = σ(z) = 0.710950, then L = (a − y)² = 0.168879. Each value is computed from the one before it and kept.
  3. The dial turns to 0.710 and a nudge of +0.010 leaves it. Through z = wx + b it doubles: Δz = +0.020 (z reads 0.920). Through σ it shrinks about five-fold: Δa = +0.004093 (a reads 0.715042). Through (a − y)² it shrinks a little more: ΔL = +0.003380 (L reads 0.172260).
  4. Measured: 0.003380 per 0.010, so ΔL/Δw = 0.338046 per unit of w. That is the number the backward pass has to reproduce.
  5. The dial returns to 0.700 and every value returns with it; the ghosts of the nudge stay. The backward pass leaves L and walks back the last edge: its slope is 2(a − y) = 2 × 0.410950 = 0.821899, read off the kept a.
  6. Back one more edge: the slope of σ at the kept z is a(1 − a) = 0.710950 × 0.289050 = 0.205500 — from the kept value, not the turned one.
  7. Back to w: the slope of wx + b in w is x = 2.
  8. Multiply the three: 0.821899 × 0.205500 × 2 = 0.337801, written under the measured 0.338046. They agree to three decimals. A finite difference is not the derivative; it is the derivative plus a term that shrinks with Δw.
  9. Nothing was updated: w is where it started. Forward computes and keeps; backward reuses and multiplies.
