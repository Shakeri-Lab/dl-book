Scope and caveats

Nothing here is trained and no accuracy is claimed; the chapter's “about 90%” belongs to its own seeded run, whose points, \(\phi_\theta\) and boundary coordinate it does not print and this panel therefore does not reproduce. The moon shape is the chapter's own parametrisation sampled at sixteen equally spaced angles per moon with the radius alternating 1.07 and 0.93. The hidden weights and biases, the readout, and the second drawing direction are declared above and held fixed; only \(\lambda\), the slope on the negative side of the bend, moves, from 1 — where the layer is affine and the stack collapses, the chapter's first equation — to 0, the chapter's rectifier. Those intermediate bends are a drawing device for continuity, not an activation the chapter trains. Because the layer is the identity on the input plane in these two directions when \(\lambda = 1\), the first frame is the input plane itself, shifted; the horizontal coordinate is the readout coordinate at every \(\lambda\), so the cut at \(-b/\lVert w\rVert\) is one fixed line and the points, not the line, do the moving. The vertical coordinate is centred on the cloud's mean at every frame, as the chapter centres its residual before taking a principal component, and it is a declared orthogonal direction rather than the chapter's measured principal component. A straight cut in this feature space is a bent, piecewise-linear boundary back in the input plane — kinked wherever a hidden unit switches on or off, which is exactly the chapter's dashed contour and exactly the point. The convex hulls of the two declared classes overlap in the input plane, so the failure in the first frame is not a badly placed line: no straight line separates them there. Check yourself. Double every readout weight and the readout bias together, leaving the hidden layer alone. Does the cut move? Does the picture?

Neither moves. The drawn coordinate is \(\hat{w}\cdot\phi\), and \(\hat{w} = w/\lVert w\rVert\) is unchanged by scaling; the cut is \(-b/\lVert w\rVert = 1/4 = 0.25\), exactly what \(0.5/2\) already gave. Doubling the readout doubles every score and changes no decision.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. Thirty-two declared points, sixteen on each moon, in the input plane, with one straight cut drawn through them. Ten sit on the wrong side. Predict whether the cut has to bend.
  2. The cut is not drawn by hand: it is the readout's own boundary, the exact coordinate minus b over the norm of w, which is 0.25 here. It will not move again.
  3. The bend begins to open. Its negative-side slope lambda leaves 1, where the layer is affine and the whole stack collapses to one linear map, and the points start to move.
  4. Each point travels a straight segment, because every drawn coordinate is affine in lambda. The cut stands where it stood, and it is still straight.
  5. The two classes separate along the readout coordinate. Points cross the stationary cut one at a time, and the wrong-side count falls.
  6. The last point crosses and the bend closes on lambda equal to zero: the chapter's rectifier, and the learned feature space of its figure.
  7. The same straight cut now lies in a clear channel, with every point of both classes on its own side of it.
  8. Grey paths recall where the ten crossers started. These are the same thirty-two points and the same straight line; only the space between them was re-drawn.
