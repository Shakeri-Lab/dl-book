Watch the mechanism: winning a step can lose the sequence

If A is the more likely first token, must the most likely complete sequence start with A?

Scope and caveats

Blue marks the supplied history, green the model's probabilities. Width counts alternatives at one depth, not tokens generated at once. One requested answer uses the best candidate; the chapter's code keeps the ranked list for inspection. An other stub groups unexpanded alternatives; its mass bounds every completion below it. All such bounds are below this example's winner. EOS contributes to the joint score and stops that sequence. Scores are not renormalized over the beam. Finite-width beam search need not find the global optimum. This picture shows search decisions, not computation time.

  4. After A x, EOS has probability 0.80. The complete joint probability is 0.264. Inspect the discarded counterfactual B y EOS: its three factors give 0.361. The model favored a completion greedy never visited.
  5. Reset the search frontier, not the predictor. Every edge is unchanged. This time allow two partial sequences to remain alive.
  6. The two root candidates A and B both survive, with joint probabilities 0.600 and 0.400. They are two alternatives at depth one, not two consecutive tokens. Width and depth measure different directions in the tree.
  7. Extend each prefix by one token. Blue history A supplies the context for x at 0.55; B supplies the context for y at 0.95. You cannot move that 0.95 to the A branch. The named prefixes B y and A x have joint probabilities 0.380 and 0.330. Every omitted prefix is bounded by its branch's total mass: 0.270 below A, or 0.020 below B. None can displace the two named prefixes. An omitted completion cannot gain probability by adding more conditional factors.
  8. Include EOS and rank the completions: B y EOS scores 0.361; A x EOS scores 0.264. The remaining new other masses are 0.066 and 0.019. All omitted-branch bounds are below 0.361, so the illustrated winner is certified without inventing the omitted subtrees. Both named candidates finish.
