Watch the mechanism: counting the window's stops

A window 3 wide slides along a row of 8. With a border of one zero on each side and a stride of 2, how many stops does it make?

The code cell's three regimes, one row at a time: the formula works on each axis separately, so one dimension is the whole story.

\( n_{\text{out}} = \left\lfloor \frac{\class{os-travel}{n + 2p - k}}{\class{os-step}{s}} \right\rfloor \class{os-start}{+\,1} \)

Travel, divided by the step and floored, counts the hops; plus one counts where the window starts.

The calculation is readable without playback. Opening this panel loads the optional controls.

One row stands for one axis: a 2-D map runs the same count along its height and its width, which is why the code cell prints square shapes.

Scope and caveats

The row of 8, the window of 3 and the three (padding, stride) pairs are the shapes cell's; the counts 6, 8 and 4 are what that cell prints for the formula and for torch. The values inside the row do not matter to the count, so none are drawn.

The border is zeros, drawn dashed: padding adds positions for the window to stand on, not information. With stride 2 the last padded position is never a window start, and the floor is exactly that: a leftover shorter than one step buys no further stop.

Blue is the input, green the outputs, and the window and the ruler are neutral ink: nothing here is learned.

Check yourself. A 28-wide map meets a window of 3 with a border of 1 and stride 2. How many outputs along that axis?

14: the padded length is 30, the window can travel 27, so ⌊27 / 2⌋ + 1 = 13 + 1 = 14. The floor drops the last half step, and stride 2 halves the map, as a pooling layer would.

  6. The window hops 2 at a time: 7 divided by 2 is 3 whole hops, plus the start, 4 outputs. The last padded cell is never a window start; the floor drops it.
