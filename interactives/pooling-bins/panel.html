Watch the mechanism: the shift that pooling cannot see

Both clues move one pixel right — does the pooled map change? And one pixel down?

The 4 × 4 grid is this chapter's pool-invariance cell, printed above; blank cells are 0. The right shift is the chapter's shifted. The down shift is this panel's own computed case.

The calculation is readable without playback. Opening this panel loads the optional controls.

Only the two clues move, on the timeline rather than under a control; the bins, the window and the rule are fixed, and no kernel is learned, which is why nothing here is drawn in the colour reserved for a learnable parameter. The equality is this construction's, as the chapter says: each isolated maximum stayed inside its 2 × 2 window. It is not translation invariance in general. The one-pixel move down is this panel's own computed case, made by applying the same 2 × 2 max-pool to the same two clues; the chapter does not print it. Under that move the 9 crosses from the upper-left bin into the lower-left one and the 3 does not, so exactly two of the four pooled values change. Tolerance is local and alignment-dependent, not invariance. How much tolerance pooling buys in a trained network is measured at the end of this chapter, not assumed here.
