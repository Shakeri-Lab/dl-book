Scope and caveats

The chapter's coefficients are 1, −2, 1. That is why nothing on the picture is drawn in the colour reserved for a learnable parameter: the one control this panel has turns the middle coefficient by hand, in ink, and no optimizer ever touches it. The pale green shape standing from the first frame is the declared bump drawn ahead of its own construction — a target to aim at, not a second measurement — which is why it is the same green as the sum that aims at it; it stays while the control can still miss it, and only its dashed edge disappears, under the sum, when the sum lands. The control runs from −3 to +1 in steps of 0.25, and the timeline sweeps it from +1, a bare ramp, down to −2. Every sum drawn for a value other than −2, its three slopes +1, 1 + c, 2 + c, and the value 4.5 (2 + c) it reaches at the right edge are this panel's declared computed variants, not numbers of the chapter; the chapter writes one coefficient, −2, and one bump. The finished bump has four straight pieces, of slopes 0, +1, −1, 0; the ledger above the plot writes the last three and leaves the first, the flat zero before any hinge, to the picture. That −2 is forced rests on the equal spacing of the three breakpoints and on the outer coefficients both being 1: the last slope, 2 + c, and the height at the last breakpoint, 4 + 2c, vanish together only there. One bump is a construction, not an approximation theorem: how many hinges a given target needs, and whether training finds them, are the chapter's next questions and not this panel's claim.

2, −4, 2. Scaling every coefficient scales the whole function: the peak becomes 4 and the slopes 0, +2, −2, 0, so the sum is still exactly zero outside the window.

  2. A pen draws their plain sum on in green, from the left. As it crosses each breakpoint that ramp's coefficient, +1, is written beside its name and the slope of the sum on the piece that follows is written above the piece: +1, then +2, then +3. Each ramp adds its coefficient to the running slope, and the sum runs out of the top of the plot.
  3. A hold. The middle coefficient is the one number the control under the picture turns; it stands at +1. Which value would bring the sum back to zero and keep it there? Decide before playing on — or drag the control and find out.
  4. The coefficient sweeps down from +1. The dashed ramp, which is always c times h₂, swings down through the axis, and the sum's last two pieces swing with it: the middle piece about the apex, the tail about the axis. With too little the tail still climbs away; the sweep ends where the tail lies flat on zero.
  5. It lands at −2, and only there: the ledger reads +1, −1, 0, because +1 − 2 + 1 = 0, and the sum lies on the target, whose fill deepens. The peak height 2 is written at the apex, which is g(0.5) = 2; a bracket under the ticks marks [−1.5, 2.5], the only place the sum is nonzero; the identity g(x) = h₁ − 2 h₂ + h₃ appears; and the ramps fade to a ghost. The four pieces, in order, have slopes 0, +1, −1, 0. Drag the control past −2 and the tail dives below zero for ever; stop short and it climbs away.
