Watch the mechanism: active forward, quiet backward

Can a neuron have a large output and still pass almost no backward sensitivity?

Watch one activation component in both directions. Its face is its own activation curve: the operating point rides that curve, and the tangent drawn there is the multiplier a backward unit probe meets. The timeline moves z; you can drag it too.

Scope and caveats

Filled area is proportional to that sensitivity; the hollow ring only locates a signal that may be too small to see. The component's face shows its activation function on that function's own vertical scale (sigmoid from 0 to 1, ReLU from 0 to 6), so a tangent's tilt compares slopes within one face, not across the two; the printed multiplier is the number. The z slider covers the chapter's plotted domain, from −6 to 6: every value at a dragged z is computed from the chapter's formulas, not printed in it. The ten-component path isolates best-case sigmoid derivative factors, not ten simulated forward activations. Its ruler is a logarithmic scale, a drawing device: multiplying by one quarter is one equal step of log10(¼), and the active-ReLU mark stays at 1 because each active factor is one. Weight matrices and branching are omitted from this ceiling, not assumed harmless: they also affect the complete backward recursion. Active ReLU contributes one locally; negative inputs contribute zero. At its nondifferentiable zero, PyTorch chooses zero for backpropagation, a convention and not a slope, so no tangent is drawn there. Nothing is trained or updated.

Check yourself. At z = −6 the sigmoid's output is about 0.0025: a quiet neuron. What is its backward factor?

About 0.0025 again: the sigmoid's slope at −6 equals its slope at +6, 0.002467. The slope is what matters, and the S is flat at both ends. Sensitivity peaks at z = 0, where the factor is exactly ¼.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. The picture is an activation component inside a network: the weighted sum supplies pre-activation z; the component emits a for the next layer. The component's face is its own activation curve between two dotted rails, the output's floor and ceiling, with no grid. At z=0 the operating point sits mid-curve and the sigmoid emits one half.
  2. Keep that forward value cached. A tangent appears at the operating point, tied by a dashed link to the multiplier on the backward lane, which reads "times slope". A normalized unit sensitivity then travels from the output side toward the input side. This probe isolates the activation derivative; it does not cross an uncomputed weight matrix. The slope stays withheld until the probe meets the multiplier.
  3. At the component the filled signal area becomes one quarter of its incoming area. The derivative at zero is exactly one quarter even though the manuscript's even-sized 300-point plotting grid does not sample zero. Then z rises toward six: the point rides up the curve, the output grows, the tangent tilts flat, and the multiplier returns to a question mark.
  4. At z=6 the sigmoid output is approximately 0.9975 and the tangent is nearly flat. Predict the backward multiplier before watching the probe return.
  5. The same unit probe is now multiplied by approximately 0.002467. The large forward output stays visible. A large activation does not imply a large local derivative: activity and sensitivity are different quantities. Dragging z to minus six shows the other side: output near zero and the same tiny slope, quiet forward and quiet backward.
  6. Substitute ReLU at the same positive input. Its face is a line with an explicit vertex at zero; its output is six and its slope is one, so the unit backward probe passes unchanged. For a negative input the slope is zero and nothing is delivered. At the kink no tangent is drawn: PyTorch's zero there is a convention, not a classical derivative. This is not a guarantee about surrounding weights.
  7. Return to the sigmoid witness and isolate only the sigmoid factors along a longer backward path. The numbered components count factors in reverse traversal order, not measured network layers. Each is given its largest possible multiplier, one quarter; the weight-matrix contributions are outside this calculation. Under the path a logarithmic ruler runs from 1 on the right past ten to the minus six on the left. Each crossing moves its marker one equal step left, because multiplying by a constant is an equal step on a log scale. A second mark, the active-ReLU path, stays at 1.
  8. Ten such factors give at most 1/1,048,576, approximately 9.54 times ten to the minus seven: ten equal steps, ending just past the ten-to-the-minus-six tick. The filled signal is still positive even when it becomes subpixel; its outline is only a location cue. This ceiling is not a bound on the complete gradient.
