Watch the mechanism: active forward, quiet backward

Can a neuron have a large output and still pass almost no backward sensitivity?

Watch the same activation component in both directions. Its forward value stays visible while a unit backward probe passes through its local derivative. The small curves explain the component; they are not plots of signal decay.

\( \residualpart{g_z}=\residualpart{g_a}\,\class{dg-local-factor}{f'(z)} \) \( \sigma'(z)\leq\class{dg-quarter}{\tfrac14},\quad \operatorname{ReLU}'(z)=\class{dg-open}{1}\ (z>0) \) \( \prod_{j=1}^{k}\sigma'(z_j)\leq\class{dg-quarter}{(\tfrac14)^k} \)

Ten sigmoid factors: below one millionth, not zero. Weight matrices and branching still affect the full gradient.

The calculation is readable without playback. Opening this panel loads the optional controls.

The unit probe measures a local sensitivity, not an observed loss gradient.

Scope and caveats

Filled area is proportional to that sensitivity; the hollow ring only locates a signal that may be too small to see. The ten-component path isolates best-case sigmoid derivative factors, not ten simulated forward activations. Weight matrices and branching are omitted from this ceiling, not assumed harmless: they also affect the complete backward recursion. Active ReLU contributes one locally; negative inputs contribute zero. At its nondifferentiable zero, PyTorch chooses zero for backpropagation. Nothing is trained or updated.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. The main picture is an activation component inside a network: the weighted sum supplies pre-activation z; the component emits a for the next layer. At z=0, sigmoid emits one half. The two small plots show activation functions, with their own vertical axes, not accumulated gradient decay.
  2. Keep that forward value cached. Send a normalized unit sensitivity from the output side backward toward the input side. This probe isolates the activation derivative; it does not cross an uncomputed weight matrix.
  3. At the component, the filled signal area becomes one quarter of its incoming area. The derivative at zero is exactly one quarter even though the manuscript's even-sized 300-point plotting grid does not sample zero. The forward output is still one half.
  4. Move z to six. The sigmoid output rises to approximately 0.9975. Its small inset marks a nearly flat tangent. Predict the backward transmission before watching the probe return.
  5. The same unit probe is now multiplied by approximately 0.002467. The large forward output stays visible. A large activation does not imply a large local derivative: activity and sensitivity are different quantities.
  6. Substitute ReLU at the same positive input. Its output is six and its local derivative is one, so the unit backward probe passes unchanged. This is not a guarantee about surrounding weights or inactive ReLU units.
  7. Return to the sigmoid witness and isolate only the sigmoid factors along a longer backward path. The numbered components count factors in reverse traversal order, not measured network layers. Each is given its largest possible multiplier, one quarter. The weight-matrix contributions are outside this calculation.
  8. Ten such factors give at most 1/1,048,576, approximately 9.54 times ten to the minus seven. The filled signal is still positive even when it becomes subpixel; its outline is only a location cue. This ceiling is not a bound on the complete gradient. The manuscript's separate experiment measures actual networks.
