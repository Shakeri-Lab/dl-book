These are shapes, not values: the boxes show how big each tensor is, and nothing here is trained or evaluated.

Scope and caveats

Every shape comes from the lenet cell's layers and matches its shape comments: the output-size formula gives the height and width, and the number of kernels gives the depth. The chapter's prose rounds the size path to 28, 14, 5; the unpadded second convolution's 10 × 10 sits between 14 and 5.

A box's face is drawn in proportion to height and width and its depth to the number of channels; the head's bars are in proportion to their length. ReLU follows each convolution and the first two dense layers without changing any shape, so it is named on the arrows and not drawn.

Blue is the input, green each computed tensor, and orange the learned weights' counts and shapes; the arrows are neutral ink.

Check yourself. fc1 reads the flattened summary and has 120 outputs. How many parameters does it hold?

48,120: a 120 × 400 weight matrix, one weight for each of the 400 flattened numbers and each output, plus 120 biases. That is more than eighteen times the 2,572 parameters of both convolutions together, which is the imbalance the parameter audit below prints.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. A one-channel 28 by 28 image goes in; ten votes come out. The shape is followed through every layer.
  2. conv1: six 5 by 5 kernels with padding 2. Height and width stay 28, since 28 plus 4 minus 5, plus 1, is 28. Six kernels make six maps: 6 by 28 by 28.
  3. Max pooling with a 2 by 2 window and stride 2 halves each map: 6 by 14 by 14.
  4. Predict. conv2 has sixteen 5 by 5 kernels, each 6 deep, and no padding. What size comes out of 14 by 14?
  5. 10 by 10: 14 minus 5, plus 1. Sixteen kernels make sixteen maps: 16 by 10 by 10.
  6. Pooling again gives 16 by 5 by 5. The size fell from 28 to 5 while the depth grew from 1 to 16.
  7. Flatten the 16 by 5 by 5 summary into 400 numbers. Three dense layers take it to 120, 84 and finally 10 logits, one per class.
  8. Convolve, shrink, deepen; repeat; then decide.
