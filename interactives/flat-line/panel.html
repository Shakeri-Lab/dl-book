Sixteen evenly spaced points stand in for the chapter's grid, and the sweep compares straight lines only, which is why the bend's residual survives every tilt.

Scope and caveats

The points are the curve at the midpoints t = −1 + (2j + 1)/16 for j = 0, …, 15: evenly spread and symmetric about zero. L uses the chapter's normalization, 1/(nd) with n = 16 and d = 2, and every residual in L is drawn. The line turns in whole degrees, the small plot records L at each degree the sweep has passed, and the value of L stays beside the flat line's right end while the line turns.

Every line pivots at the sample mean, because PCA centers the rows. The minimum sits at the flat line because the curve is symmetric, so its horizontal and vertical deviations have no cross term, and its horizontal spread, 0.332, exceeds its vertical spread, 0.196 (spread: the mean squared distance from the center along an axis). The chapter's cell measures its own held-out grid and prints its own mean squared error, which this sample does not reproduce digit for digit.

Blue marks the points, green their reconstructions on the line, and wine the residuals and L; orange is the line's direction, the one thing the tied autoencoder learns here, and ink marks the pivot. The chapter's figure draws its PCA line in its house orange too.

Check yourself. Double the bend: the same sixteen points on (t, 3(t² − 1/3)). Which line through the center leaves the least residual now, and what is its L?

The vertical line, L = 0.166. Doubling the height makes the vertical spread four times larger, from 0.196 to 0.784, which now exceeds the horizontal 0.332. A line leaves the spread across it as residual, so the best line now runs along the larger spread; the flat line would leave 0.392.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. Sixteen points of the planted curve sit around their center, marked with a cross. PCA's flat line runs through the center, and each point drops a residual straight up or down to its foot on the line. Together they leave L = 0.098.
  2. Predict. Turn the line about the center, keeping every residual perpendicular to it. Can any direction leave less squared residual than the flat line?
  3. The line turns toward upright. Residuals near the bottom of the curve shorten, but those of the two arms lengthen more, and L climbs to 0.166 when the line stands upright. A small plot traces L against tilt.
  4. The line keeps turning. L falls again and returns to 0.098 only when the line is flat, at 180 degrees, which closes a single hump in the plot.
  5. At a 45-degree tilt the line leans along the right arm. Its top point nearly touches the line, but the far arm's residuals grow long: L = 0.132, more than flat.
  6. Back to flat. Every tilt costs residual: the plot never dips below the flat line's L, so no line leaves less than the flat one.
  7. What is left, 0.098, is the bend itself: no straight line can follow it.
  8. PCA's flat line wins among lines; only a map that bends can do better. The tilt dial turns the line to any other direction.
