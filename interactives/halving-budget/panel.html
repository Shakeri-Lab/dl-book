Watch the mechanism: a search that spends unevenly

Twenty-seven configurations, each deserving twenty-seven epochs, is 729 epoch-units. Successive halving still takes one of them all the way to 27 epochs. What does it spend?

The interlude's successive-halving example drawn as the area it buys: configurations across the bottom, epochs up the side, and the shape the budget actually takes inside the square that training everything would fill.

\( \class{hv-rung}{n_r\,b_r} = 27 \quad\text{at every rung} \)\( 27 + 9(2) + 3(6) + 1(18) = \class{hv-total}{81} \quad\text{against}\quad \class{hv-full}{729} \)

The staircase is 81 of the square’s 729: the search spent one ninth.

The calculation is readable without playback. Opening this panel loads the optional controls.

The saving assumes early rank predicts late rank well enough; a slow-starting configuration can be pruned before its advantage appears, which is why pruning policy is part of the method and must be logged.

Scope and caveats

Nothing here is trained and no configuration is better than another: the columns are places in a budget, not measured results, and which third survives each round is left deliberately unlabelled. The surviving columns are drawn at the left only so the staircase reads as one shape; a real bracket keeps whichever configurations rank highest, scattered across the space.

The staircase is the cost with resumed checkpoints, which is the interlude's own assumption: a survivor is not retrained from scratch, so each round pays only for the epochs it adds. Without checkpoints the same schedule would pay for the whole cumulative budget every round, and the four rungs would cost 27 each rather than 27, 18, 18 and 18.

The square is the alternative the interlude names — all 27 configurations trained for 27 epochs — drawn to the same scale so the comparison is area against area. The running totals 45 and 63 are partial sums of the chapter's own cost expression, and the ratio of nine is 729 divided by 81; the chapter prints the two endpoints and not the steps between them. Grey is the budget that has been spent, and the pale outline the budget that was never needed.

Check yourself. Same rule — keep a third, triple the budget — but start from 81 configurations at one epoch. How many rounds until one survives, what budget does it reach, and what does the whole search cost?

Five rounds, 81 epochs, and 297 epoch-units. The counts run 81, 27, 9, 3, 1 and the budgets 1, 3, 9, 27, 81, so the rounds cost 81, then 54 four times over. Training all 81 for 81 epochs would cost 6561, so the saving grows to about 22 times.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. Predict. An empty square: 27 configurations across the bottom, 27 epochs up the side, so its area is the 729 epoch-units that training everything would cost. Nothing has been spent.
  2. One epoch each. A single band fills the whole width: every one of the 27 configurations runs for one epoch. Spent so far, 27.
  3. Keep a third. The frontier narrows from 27 columns to nine, and those nine grow from one epoch to a cumulative three. The round adds 18; spent so far, 45.
  4. Again. Three survive and grow from three epochs to nine. The block is a third as wide and three times as tall, so this round adds the same 18; spent so far, 63.
  5. Hold. One configuration is left, and it is about to run to the full 27 epochs. Predict the total before releasing it.
  6. The last rung. The single survivor climbs from nine epochs to 27, adding 18. The whole search cost 81 epoch-units, the number the interlude prints.
  7. Compare. The square behind the staircase lights up: 729 epoch-units for training all 27 configurations to 27 epochs.
  8. The shape of the spending. The staircase is one ninth of the square. Every rung held the same product of configurations and budget, 27, which is why each round after the first cost the same.
