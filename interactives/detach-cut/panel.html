Watch the mechanism: what crosses a truncation cut

Two lanes train on the same six-step stream, cut into chunks of three. One carries the state across the cut, detached; the other resets it to zero. What crosses the cut in each lane: the value going forward, the gradient coming back, both, or neither?

A schematic of the two finite-horizon recipes just described: truncated BPTT carries the state across each cut and detaches it there; fixed-window training starts every window from zero state.

Inside a chunk the gradient still flows back and still trains the shared weights; the cut removes only the paths that cross it.

Scope and caveats

Six steps and one cut after the third are a schematic. The recipes above carry and detach the state across chunks of roughly 20 to 50 steps, or sample 100-character windows that each start from zero state; neither length is drawn here.

In code, the carry lane is h = h.detach() between chunks, and the reset lane starts each window from a zero state.

Cutting at random points and dividing each surviving term by the probability that the cuts spared it, the unbiased alternative mentioned above, is not drawn. Clipping is a separate tool: it rescales large gradients and moves no cut.

Blue marks the carried value, wine the loss, its gradient and the rings on the states the gradient reaches, and ink the cut, the detach tag with its forward point, the zero start and the stop bar; the cells are grey. Shape and text carry the same distinctions: the value is a round packet on the line of states, the gradient a pointed packet on the rail beneath it.

Check yourself. Chunks of three steps, carried and detached, run for nine steps. The loss at step 9 depends on steps 5 and 7. Which of the two can its gradient reach?

Step 7, yes; step 5, no. The cuts fall after steps 3, 6 and 9, so steps 7, 8 and 9 share one chunk and the gradient of step 9's loss stops at the cut after step 6. Step 5's value still reaches step 9 through the carried state.

Transcript and keyboard help
  1. Two lanes run the same stream of six steps, cut after step 3. The upper lane carries the state across the cut through a detach tag; the lower lane starts step 4 again from a zero box. Step 2's input is marked. Before playing on: what crosses the cut in each lane, the value going forward, the gradient coming back, both, or neither?
  2. Step 2's input enters the state and rides to step 3 in both lanes, then holds there.
  3. In the carry lane the value passes through the detach tag, crosses the cut and rides on to step 6; the states it reaches are shaded. In the reset lane it halts at the cut and fades to a ring, and step 4 starts again from zero.
  4. The loss at step 6 appears in both lanes. Before playing on: how far back does its gradient travel in each lane?
  5. The gradient runs back along the rail under the states, through steps 6, 5 and 4 in both lanes, and stops at the cut, where a short bar ends it. Each state it reaches wears a wine ring; steps 1 to 3 get none.
  6. At the cut, the carry lane reads “value crosses” and “gradient stops”; the reset lane reads “value stops” and “gradient stops”. Detach passes the value but not the gradient; reset passes neither. The detach tag gains a forward point: a one-way gate.
  7. The carry lane alone: step 6 uses step 2's value, carried through the cut, yet the gradient of step 6's loss stops at the cut and never reaches step 3 or step 2, so it cannot teach the model to keep that value.
  8. The values cross the boundary; the gradient graph does not.
