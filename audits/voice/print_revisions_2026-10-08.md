For an input stack $X$ with $C_{\text{in}}$ channels, output feature map $o \in \{1, \dots, C_{\text{out}}\}$ is $$ [H_o]_{i,j} \;=\; b_o \;+\; \sum_{c=1}^{C_{\text{in}}}\; \Bigl(\,\sum_{u=-\Delta}^{\Delta}\;\sum_{v=-\Delta}^{\Delta} [V_{o,c}]_{u,v}\,[X_c]_{i+u,\,j+v}\Bigr), $$ {#eq-conv-layer} and a pointwise activation follows; as in @sec-07-filters-convolution, the kernel is $k \times k$ with $k = 2\Delta + 1$. The inner double sum is the cross-correlation of @eq-crosscorr, applied to input channel $c$ with the slice $V_{o,c}$ of the kernel; the outer sum adds the $C_{\text{in}}$ results, and $b_o$ is the bias of output channel $o$. The computation for one output pixel is one dot product between a $C_{\text{in}} \times k \times k$ patch of the input and the $C_{\text{in}} \times k \times k$ kernel of output channel $o$, plus the bias $b_o$. A dense layer of an MLP takes a dot product across all of its input features; a convolutional layer takes the same kind of dot product across all input channels but only a small spatial window, and slides it over the grid.
